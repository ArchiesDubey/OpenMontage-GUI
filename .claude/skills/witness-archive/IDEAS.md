## E01 — The light died, the poison didn't  ⭐ IN PRODUCTION

> **Premise corrected during research (2026-07-28).** The working title was "the dial that
> still glows." It doesn't. The zinc-sulfide phosphor is destroyed by the very radiation
> exciting it and burns out in ~5 years; the radium's half-life is 1,600 years. The dial in
> the museum case went dark around 1923 and is still dangerously radioactive. Nearly all
> competing coverage gets this wrong — which makes it both the honest framing and the
> strongest differentiator.

- **Object:** a wristwatch dial hand-painted in 1918, dark and unremarkable in its museum
  case, still radioactive — and the exhumed bones of the women who painted it, which *do*
  still register a century on.
- **Territory:** dark medicine + industrial negligence
- **Named:** Grace Fryer, Katherine Schaub, Quinta McDonald, Amelia Maggia; Dr Harrison
  Martland; the US Radium Corporation's own physicians
- **The turn:** the company's medical examinations that found the illness and reported the
  women healthy anyway
- **Where it is now:** museum collections; the graves; the still-contaminated Orange, NJ site
- **Monetisation:** safe — no gore required
- **Why it earns in 3 years:** foundational occupational-safety story, permanent search demand
- **Why it's episode ONE:** the subject literally rhymes with the character. A story about a
  substance that glows in the dark, told by a figure carrying a lantern. The format's
  signature explains itself in the first ten seconds.

## E02 — Slotin's screwdriver  · queued

- **Object:** a flat-bladed screwdriver holding apart two beryllium hemispheres around a

- **Territory:** dark medicine / institutional

- **The turn:** the procedure had already killed Daghlian; Slotin kept doing it by hand
- **Where it is now:** the core was melted down into the stockpile
- **Monetisation:** safe · **Note:** more covered than E01 on faceless channels
